## Spatially Informed and Direction Sensitive Representation Learning for Spatial Transcriptomics

**Authors:** Azim Dehghani Amirabad, Ali Khodabandeh Yalabadi, Artem Moskalev, Yun-Ching Chen, Mehdi Pirooznia, Scott Oloff, Rui Liao

**Primary category:** q-bio.QM

**arXiv:** [2610.28579v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28579v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-20

**Keyword hits:** foundation model

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Cellular states are shaped by both intrinsic gene expression programs and the spatial microenvironment in which cells reside. Spatial transcriptomics makes this context measurable, but effective representation learning requires capturing both spatially informative molecular signals and intercellular organization. Existing approaches address these challenges only partially: conventional gene selection can overlook spatially structured signals, while methods for encoding cell-cell relationships often either reduce a cell's microenvironment to proximity alone, discarding direction, or rely on absolute coordinates tied to an arbitrary tissue frame. Consequently, cells with similar neighbor distances but distinct spatial organization may receive overly similar representations, despite experiencing different biological contexts that shape their cellular states. We introduce Tessera, a spatial representation learning framework that addresses both molecular selection and cellular context. Tessera combines spatially informed gene selection with a geometric inductive bias that contextualizes each cell within its local tissue environment. By modeling relative cell-cell relationships, Tessera is translation-invariant while preserving direction, allowing neighboring cells to contribute differently according to their spatial context and cellular state. Across held-out tissues and technologies, Tessera outperforms existing spatial foundation models in representation quality and generalizes zero-shot to contexts up to $16\times$ larger than those seen during training.

## A Bayesian machine learning approach to direct and spillover effects in cluster-randomized trials with noncompliance

**Authors:** Saurabh Bhandari, Fan Li, Michael J. Daniels

**Primary category:** stat.ME

**arXiv:** [2610.27425v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27425v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-20

**Keyword hits:** g-computation

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

We develop a Bayesian machine learning approach for estimating direct and spillover effects in cluster-randomized trials with treatment noncompliance. To flexibly model outcomes and treatment receipt while accounting for within-cluster dependence, we combine Bayesian additive regression trees with cluster-level random effects. We augment the outcome regression with a peer-treatment clever covariate, defined as the predicted mean proportion of treated peers from a preliminary receipt model, to mitigate regularization-induced distortion of the effect decomposition. Within a Bayesian g-computation framework, we estimate cluster-average intent-to-treat effects and cluster-average local average treatment effects and examine sensitivity to the cross-world restrictions underlying their decomposition. Simulation studies show reduced bias under model misspecification, while the correctly specified parametric models show expected frequentist properties. We find that the clever covariate improves the decomposition of the total effect into direct and spillover components under moderate or strong spillover, with similar gains in smaller clusters. Its benefits vary with spillover strength, and the $95\%$ credible intervals remain conservative. An application to the Primary School Deworming Project in Kenya suggests reduced infection risk, with a larger estimated contribution from individual treatment receipt than from peer receipt. Overall, our approach provides a flexible Bayesian estimation of direct and spillover effect decomposition in the presence of treatment noncompliance.
