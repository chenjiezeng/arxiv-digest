## HELICS: Biobank-scale Conditional Synthetic Genome Generation via Latent Flow Matching

**Authors:** Ahmad Abdel-Azim, Xihong Lin

**Primary category:** q-bio.GN

**arXiv:** [2610.36361v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36361v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-26

**Keyword hits:** uk biobank, biobank

**Score:** 2

**Abstract:**

Individual-level genotype data underpin genetic discovery, disease-risk modeling, and therapeutic development, yet many critical settings remain data-limited, including underrepresented ancestries, rare or imbalanced disease cohorts, and restricted-access biobanks. Conditional synthetic genome generation would support privacy-aware benchmarking, simulation, and data augmentation, but requires learning an ultra-high-dimensional discrete distribution over hundreds of thousands to tens of millions of correlated single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), or sites of DNA variation. We introduce HELICS: High-fidelity gEneration via Latent Interpolant Conditional Synthesis, a phasing-free latent flow matching framework for genome-wide SNP-level generation from biobank-scale genotype data. HELICS tokenizes chromosomes with convolutional autoencoders, reducing >600K SNPs to 4,096 continuous latent tokens, and trains a transformer parameterized conditional flow matching model over the concatenated multi-chromosome latent representation. Trained on over 417K genomes in the UK Biobank, HELICS generates genome-wide common-variant synthetic cohorts without haplotype phasing and achieves stronger fidelity to real genotypes than existing haplotype-based simulators, including high allele-frequency agreement in external evaluation and superior preservation of local and long-range SNP correlation structure. Conditioning on ancestry and genetic liability summaries enables targeted generation across genetic risk profiles; in perturbation analyses, by increasing the conditioned disease trait liability, HELICS induces SNP-level dosage changes in generated genomes that are highly correlated with published association weights. HELICS provides a scalable generative modeling framework for SNP-level genome data and a path toward conditional synthetic cohorts for data-limited genetic studies.

## Personal preference or system role? Battery swapping among UK electric vehicle users

**Authors:** Janak Parmar, Huseyin Ayan, Dilum Dissanayake, Margaret Bell

**Primary category:** econ.EM

**arXiv:** [2610.37269v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37269v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-27

**Keyword hits:** latent class

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Battery swapping replaces a depleted EV battery with a charged one, reducing waiting time and enabling off-peak charging. Evidence on acceptance, however, remains concentrated in Asian markets, and most studies examine individual adoption intentions. This study asks whether current UK EV users judge swapping differently as a purchase option and as a technology for the transport system. Using Behavioural Reasoning Theory, an integrated choice and latent variable model, and latent class analysis, we analyse perceived usefulness, energy-system concerns, practical barriers, and satisfaction with current charging. Usefulness and energy-system concerns relate more strongly to expectations about the system. Battery compatibility and station coverage relate more strongly to personal preference, while spare battery costs relate to expectations about network viability. User groups differ in how they balance benefits, barriers, and satisfaction with existing charging. Policy should address driver barriers and network viability as distinct but connected priorities.

## Finite-population inference for cross-fitted estimators of treatment contrasts in stratified multistage surveys

**Authors:** M. Ehsan Karim

**Primary category:** stat.ME

**arXiv:** [2610.36402v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36402v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-26

**Keyword hits:** double machine learning

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Health surveys often sample clusters within strata and then individuals within clusters, and flexible outcome and treatment models complicate inference for adjusted contrasts from such surveys. We establish finite-population limit theory for survey-weighted augmented inverse-probability-weighted estimation within the double machine learning framework, using cross-fitting that keeps whole clusters together; a cross-fitted targeted maximum likelihood estimator inherits the results under additional targeting conditions. With first-stage clusters drawn by simple random sampling without replacement within strata, the theory allows arbitrary within-cluster dependence, appreciable sampling fractions, and many small strata, a few large strata, or both. Under bounded cluster sizes, a cluster-generation model and nuisance convergence-rate conditions, the estimator is asymptotically normal about the finite-population contrast, and a stability property derived from the cluster model controls the cross-fitting error without entropy conditions. The stratified ultimate-cluster variance estimator, without a first-stage finite-population correction, consistently estimates a with-replacement variance that is at least the without-replacement design variance of the normal limit, so the resulting intervals are asymptotically conservative, and exact when the excess is negligible. In simulations, near-nominal or higher coverage coexisted, in different settings, with remainder diagnostics above the study's negligibility benchmark and with inaccurate standard errors. In three Service Provision Assessment surveys from Malawi and Haiti, each frame treated as a fixed population and resampled 500 times, mean reported standard errors were 4%-20% larger than the empirical standard deviations, while coverage of whole-frame reference values was near or above nominal.

## Relaxed MEMs: Enhancing Borrowing in Multisource Exchangeability Models for Small Sample Applications

**Authors:** Shannon D. Thomas, Alexander M. Kaizer

**Primary category:** stat.AP

**arXiv:** [2610.36222v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36222v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-26

**Keyword hits:** cystic fibrosis

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Information sharing in clinical trials provides a promising way to increase trial efficiency, specifically in rare disease settings where sample sizes are small. Multisource Exchangeability Modeling (MEM) is a method that incorporates historical data from potentially multiple data sources, such as past trials, by exploring all possible configurations of exchangeability and provides a weight estimate for each configuration, allowing the user to quantify how much is being borrowed from each source. While MEMs have previously been shown to be asymptotically consistent, small sample sizes introduce uncertainty that naturally limits the amount of borrowing MEMs can facilitate. The proposed ``relaxed MEMs'' provide a mathematical framework for relaxing or removing those limits and allowing greater borrowing even in small-sample settings. Several relaxed MEM versions are proposed, and all strategies provide enhanced borrowing with minimal sacrifice in the bias and variance of the mean estimates. Specifically, the maximum absolute mean bias was 3.5\% (0.035) of the true mean in the two source continuous outcome settings and 6.4\% (0.032) of the true proportion in the two source binary outcome settings (absolute mean bias was 1.4\% (0.014) and 3.6\% (0.018), respectively, for traditional MEMs) and variance decreased relative to traditional MEMs across all exchangeable and small difference scenarios. The methods were then demonstrated using two endpoints from a series of phase II trials in cystic fibrosis.
