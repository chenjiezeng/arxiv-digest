## How Deep is the Signal? Time-Context up to 30 Years for Colorectal Cancer Prediction in a Primary-Care EHR Model

**Authors:** Achille Raviez, Lan Wang, Brendan Delaney

**Primary category:** cs.LG

**arXiv:** [2611.07265v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07265v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-05

**Keyword hits:** foundation model, ehr foundation

**Score:** 2

**Abstract:**

EHR foundation models read medical events within a fixed context window, and long context has so far been measured only in tokens. How far back in time a model must look to capture the earliest predictive signal is unknown. We introduce time-context, the calendar lookback window before the prediction point, and provide, to our knowledge, the first systematic evaluation of decade-scale time-context for an EHR foundation model. We pretrained a decoder-only transformer (34.6M parameters) on 1.4B coded events from 3.6M primary-care patients in North West London and fine-tuned it to predict incident colorectal cancer under a 12-month pre-diagnostic buffer. A frozen cohort of 1,008 cases and 9,582 matched controls, all registered for at least 30 years, was evaluated at 15 nested lookback windows from 2 to 30 years, so that every truncation is compared within the same patients. Discrimination rose from an AUROC of 0.551 at 2 years to 0.590 at 18 years, then plateaued, with about 80% of the gain reached within 10 years; an XGBoost baseline converged to the same level (peak AUROC 0.589). A simulation further suggests that rotary position embeddings (RoPE) over elapsed time alias beyond about 20 years at the default base frequency, motivating a retuned $θ$. Time-context is a measurable design axis for clinical prediction models, distinct from token-context, and should be reported.

## Reclassification Is All You Missed: Surfacing Variant Reinterpretation Need in Online Health Communities at Population Scale

**Authors:** Kamalakkannan Ravi, Mengshu Nie, Annapurna Poduri, Cong Liu

**Primary category:** cs.CL

**arXiv:** [2611.07054v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07054v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-04

**Keyword hits:** clinvar

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Genetic variants are routinely reclassified in ClinVar after a patient is tested, sometimes from uncertain to pathogenic, yet patient recontact is inconsistent, with no clear duty and responsibility diffused among labs, providers, and patients. Many affected people discuss their results in public online health communities that clinical recontact often does not reach. We built PRISM, an agentic large-language-model pipeline that screens these posts, checks whether the variant was later reclassified, and drafts a disclaimer-bearing outreach message, in a no-contact dry run. Across 3.9 million posts from 32 communities, agent-predicted reinterpretation-relevant discussion is rare (0.288%) but concentrated in specialist genetics communities (6.30% versus 0.10% in general medical-advice communities) and growing fivefold since 2014; PRISM surfaces 123 candidate classification differences, 5 of them from an uncertain submission to pathogenic or likely pathogenic. On a held-out set of 30 real-post drafts, a clinician judged 27 factually grounded and 15 fit to send: factual accuracy is necessary but not sufficient for clinically acceptable outreach. PRISM contributes a population-scale reinterpretation-need map with code and data to be released upon publication.
