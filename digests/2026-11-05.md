## Efficient estimation of regression models for recurrent events in presence of terminal events

**Authors:** Giuliana Cortese, Thomas H. Scheike

**Primary category:** stat.ME

**arXiv:** [2611.06431v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06431v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-04

**Keyword hits:** inverse probability

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Recurrent events are common in biomedical studies, and efficient methods are needed to analyse them, particularly when a terminal event may occur. Regression modelling of the marginal mean of recurrent events provides a simply interpretable approach, but standard estimation based on inverse probability of censoring weighting may not fully exploit observed event histories in multiple events settings. We demonstrate how the popular Ghosh-Lin proportional means model can be estimated efficiently by developing an augmentation procedure that is also applicable to composite endpoints. Using semiparametric efficiency theory, we characterize the fully efficient estimator. First, we investigate its construction under frailty models. We then propose a dynamic censoring-augmented estimator that is straightforward to compute and does not require a fully specified joint model for recurrent and terminal events. We show theoretically that it improves on the standard Ghosh-Lin estimator and derive an explicit expression for its asymptotic variance reduction. We also establish conditions under which tests of no treatment effect remain valid in randomized trials when the proportional means model is misspecified. Extensive simulations examine performance under dependence between recurrent and terminal events, unobserved heterogeneity, censoring-model misspecification, and auxiliary covariates. The practical gain of the proposed methods is illustrated with an analysis of catheter-related bloodstream infections in patients with chronic intestinal failure receiving home parenteral support.

## Bayesian Superpopulation Models for Population-Based Diabetes Surveillance Using Multi-Site Electronic Health Record Data

**Authors:** Rebecca Anthopolos, Bo Cai, Lorna E. Thorpe, Hui Xie, Xiaochun Li, Sarah Conderino, David C. Lee, Hui Shao, Ibrahim Zaganjor, Marc Rosenman, H. Lester Kirchner, Muchiri E. Wandai, Levon Utidjian, Tessa Crume, Kristi Reynolds, Jasmin Divers, DiCAYA Study Group

**Primary category:** stat.ME

**arXiv:** [2611.05661v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05661v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-03

**Keyword hits:** electronic health records

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Electronic health records (EHRs) are increasingly used for disease surveillance. However, data collected in EHRs may not be a representative sample of the target population for inference. Methods for EHR-based surveillance remain in their nascence. In addition to simple weighting approaches, researchers have used multilevel regression and poststratification (MRP), a popular technique for finite population quantity estimation that remains understudied in EHRs. We are motivated by a US-based collaborative to modernize diabetes surveillance using EHR data from different health system centers across the country. To estimate type 1 diabetes (T1D) prevalence in children and adolescents, we propose and evaluate three Bayesian superpopulation models that are distinguished by how they account for two important features of EHR data: unobserved heterogeneity and data sparsity. The three models include an analysis of variance-inspired approach; a model with geographic effects varying by demographic subgroup; and MRP, which we consider as the reference model. We apply the models to estimate prevalence overall and in demographic- and geographic-based subgroups. Using plasmode simulations, we evaluate model estimation performance while preserving the complexity of the EHR data. Our results show that the superpopulation-based prevalence estimates were higher than those observed in the EHR sample alone, with the magnitude of the differences varying by health system center. The bias and coverage probabilities of MRP markedly worsened when the EHR sample size was smaller or the selection mechanism was more complex. Overall, the superpopulation model based on analysis of variance demonstrated the best estimation performance.
