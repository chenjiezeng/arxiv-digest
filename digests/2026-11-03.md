## One-Sample Mendelian Randomization and Instrumental Variable Analyses in R and Stata: The OneSampleMR and ivonesamplemr Packages

**Authors:** Tom Palmer, Nicolai Vitt, Eleanor Sanderson

**Primary category:** stat.ME

**arXiv:** [2611.02887v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02887v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-02

**Keyword hits:** mendelian randomization

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Mendelian randomization (MR) uses genetic variants as instrumental variables (IVs) to estimate the causal effect of an exposure on an outcome. When individual-level data on the instruments, exposure, and outcome are available in a single sample (one-sample MR), a range of IV estimators can be used beyond the standard two-stage least squares estimator, including structural mean models (SMMs) and two-stage predictor substitu- tion (TSPS) and two-stage residual inclusion (TSRI) estimators with nonlinear second stage models. These estimators are important when the outcome is binary, where in- vestigators typically wish to report causal risk ratios or causal odds ratios. However, implementations of these estimators are scattered across software packages and are of- ten unavailable with standard errors that account for the estimation of the first stage. We describe two packages which implement these estimators: OneSampleMR for R and ivonesamplemr for Stata. Both packages fit the multiplicative and logistic SMMs and TSPS and TSRI estimators with identity, log-additive, log-multiplicative, and logit links using the generalized method of moments (GMM), so that the standard errors account for the uncertainty in both stages of estimation. OneSampleMR additionally provides the Sanderson-Windmeijer conditional F-statistic for assessing instrument strength in models with multiple exposures, and ivonesamplemr additionally provides prefix commands for moving window and within-quantile estimation for exploring nonlinear exposure-outcome relationships. We illustrate both packages using the same simulated dataset and show that they give the same estimates as each other and as existing implementations.

## HistoTx: Early fusion of H&E images and spatial transcriptomics at varying spatial transcriptomics resolution with self-supervised learning

**Authors:** Alice Driessen, Emil Malmsten, Steffen Wolf, Bernd Illing, Isinsu Katircioglu, Adriano Martinelli, Rolf Stahel, Solange Peters, Enriqueta Felip, Sanjay Popat, Krisztian Homicsko, Raphael Gottardo, Marianna Rapsomaniki, Fei Tang

**Primary category:** cs.LG

**arXiv:** [2611.02681v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02681v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-02

**Keyword hits:** foundation model

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Spatial transcriptomics maps gene expression across tissue sections and, when integrated with whole slide images, jointly captures both morphological architecture and molecular activity to provide a rich, multimodal view of tissue biology. ML approaches combining spatial transcriptomics and WSIs typically learn a mapping from morphological features to molecular profiles via contrastive alignment or supervised fine-tuning of pathology foundation models. In both cases, transcriptomics serves as a training signal rather than a true input modality, leaving inference fundamentally image-driven. We propose an early-fusion vision transformer architecture in which transcript tokens are constructed at arbitrary spatial resolution, merged with image patch tokens, and jointly processed through a shared transformer stack for deep cross-modal interaction. Starting from a pretrained vision-only pathology foundation model, we train HistoTx by continuing self-supervised pretraining on paired image and spatial transcriptomics data and demonstrate that: 1) at inference with image only, performance remains on par with the baseline on most tasks while improving on tasks that inherently benefit from molecular knowledge, such as gene expression prediction; 2) when both modalities are available at inference, jointly providing image and transcript inputs outperforms either modality alone.

## Attribution-based diagnostics for single-cell foundation models

**Authors:** Farnoush Rezaei Jafari, Tristan Lazard, Kenza Bouzid, Shruthi Bannur, Jeremiah Wander, Ashley Mae Conard, Stephanie Hyland, Lorin Crawford, Daniel Coelho de Castro

**Primary category:** cs.LG

**arXiv:** [2611.02335v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02335v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-02

**Keyword hits:** foundation model

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Single-cell RNA foundation models (scFMs) are increasingly used for in-silico biological discovery, yet predictive performance alone does not reveal whether their decisions rely on biologically meaningful signals or model-specific shortcuts. We systematically investigate post-hoc attribution of downstream predictions across two tasks: cell-type annotation using pretrained Cell2Sentence and scGPT models, and perturbation prediction using scGPT+STATE in a controlled setting. For cell annotation, we use attribution to assess whether predictions rely on established marker genes and how their relevance varies with model architecture, expression rank, and the surrounding cellular context. For perturbation prediction, we compare attribution patterns with known regulatory ground-truth in a synthetic setting to assess what they reveal about the model's learned computations. Across these tasks, attribution reveals biological feature use, contextual interactions, contributions from different input pathways, learning progression, associations with prediction quality, and potential pitfalls that are not fully captured by standard performance metrics. Experiments with complete and limited regulatory knowledge further demonstrate that conclusions drawn from attribution depend strongly on the choice of biological reference, baseline, and null model. Together, these findings highlight the value of attribution as a flexible post-hoc diagnostic for understanding model behavior, identifying potential issues, and informing the development of scFMs.

## An auditable agentic pipeline for variant-to-gene prioritisation, validated against orthogonal functional evidence

**Authors:** Shakuntala Baichoo,  Sohail,  Sarkar, Bo Wang

**Primary category:** q-bio.GN

**arXiv:** [2611.01461v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01461v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-01

**Keyword hits:** gwas

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Assigning GWAS variants to causal genes requires evidence from multiple resources. We present V2G, an agentic pipeline that records evidence retrieval and returns ranked genes alongside the retrieved evidence. We evaluate whether language-model reranking improves gene prioritisation and whether its gains depend on the retrieved evidence or recall of known associations. On 135 held-out loci where candidate retrieval succeeded and Open Targets L2G predicts, reranking by a language model improves Precision@1 over the evidence ordering it receives, 0.793 against 0.711 (+0.081, 95% CI +0.038 to +0.129), and over nearest-gene assignment (+0.178); no difference from Open Targets L2G or from training-membership baselines was detected. Given only the trait and the candidate gene symbols, in shuffled order, the same model scores 0.864 on these loci (three seeds, 0.859 - 0.867) and names the reference gene at 0.594 of the loci where the pipeline never retrieved it; with each gene's symbol moved to another gene's evidence row it follows the top-scored row and scores 0.284. The gain over the evidence ordering is recall of known genes, and the evidence as presented anchors the model to the ordering rather than informing it. Across the 296 held-out loci (five seeds each) the model keeps the given top candidate at 86% of decisions; at naturally retrieved loci its departures select a reference gene in 85% of departure decisions (31 of 37 loci), and none displaced one. Its calls carry trait-matched mouse mutant or Mendelian phenotype annotations more often than other candidates at the same locus. Train-test overlap is reported throughout. The study includes a frozen benchmark, cached predictions and reproduction code.
