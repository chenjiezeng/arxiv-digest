## Bezier-GP: Bezier-Surface-Based Gaussian Process for Chips-on-Wafer Warpage Modeling

**Authors:** Sangyup Lee, Xiao Liu, Jianjun Shi

**Primary category:** stat.AP

**arXiv:** [2611.04280v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04280v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-03

**Keyword hits:** chip

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

As the semiconductor industry transitions to the post-Moore paradigm, wafer warpage has become a critical quality issue in advanced packaging; particularly for 3D stacking and high-bandwidth memory manufacturing where die-level deformation can affect bonding quality, interconnect reliability, and yield. However, direct high-throughput measurement of individual chip warpage remains difficult in mass-production environments, while existing wafer-level metrology systems routinely provide dense 3D shape data at the wafer scale. This paper proposes a Bezier-surface-based Gaussian Process---known as Bezier-GP---for chips-on-wafer warpage modeling. The proposed framework combines wafer-level point-cloud metrology data with wafer-map layout information to construct chip-region-level Bezier surface patches, imposes continuity constraints between neighboring patches, and represents the resulting chips-on-wafer surface through a compact free-control-point vector. A GP is then introduced in this low-dimensional representation to model wafer-to-wafer variation, chip-region-level spatial dependence, and provide uncertainty quantification. Numerical experiments, using an ASC wafer-map layout, demonstrate that the proposed framework can reconstruct chips-on-wafer surfaces, preserve chip-region-level geometric interpretability, and recover population-level mean and covariance structures under a controlled multi-wafer simulation setting. The results suggest that Bezier-GP provides a measurement-driven statistical modeling foundation for connecting wafer-level metrology data to chip-region-level warpage analysis in semiconductor manufacturing.

## BEND 2.0: Benchmarking the Capabilities Needed to Design DNA

**Authors:** Andreas Bjerregaard, Rachael DeVries, Dionysia Danai Brilli, Panagiotis Antoniadis, Yan Li, Anders Krogh, Wouter Boomsma, Ole Winther, Frederikke Isa Marin

**Primary category:** q-bio.GN

**arXiv:** [2611.03806v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03806v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-02

**Keyword hits:** foundation model

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Genomic foundation models are increasingly used to propose DNA sequences and edits. But successful design requires capabilities that current benchmarks do not measure. Here we introduce BEND 2.0 (BENchmark for DNA language models), which tests how well models predict the effects of DNA changes and whether these predictions can guide sequence design. Across eight tasks built on five experimental datasets, we find that model choice and how representations are accessed both affect performance. Linear readouts fitted to frozen representations can match or exceed models' native outputs, and functional post-training substantially improves the models' capabilities. Linear readouts transfer well across experimental assays and genes, suggesting that models capture functional patterns beyond the data used to fit individual predictors. At the same time, strong prediction of average effects can conceal weaker prediction of context-specific effects, and accurate prediction of combined mutation effects does not imply accurate prediction of their interactions. In design tasks, model predictions can reduce the number of candidates needed to find a desired response, but still leave substantial room for better design choices, particularly for context-specific and multi-cell objectives. Our benchmark reveals that genomic models now display signs of general utility but have yet to saturate on performance in even simple tasks.
