### 1. Characterizing the Uncertainty, Misclassification and Inconsistency of Polygenic Prediction
**Authors:** Y. Zhang, R. Zhang, T. Ge
**Venue:** medRxiv, 2026 (preprint posted 2026-05-13)
**Source:** Scholar alert ("Lisa Bastarache — new related research", 2026-05-18)
**Thread fit:** Genetic epidemiology — PRS calibration / clinical translation.

**What the paper does (from snippet):** Quantifies (a) individual-level uncertainty in PRS estimates, (b) misclassification rates when thresholding PRS for high-risk stratification, and (c) cross-PRS inconsistency — i.e. how often two valid PRSs for the same trait disagree on who is "high risk." Pair it with the **iPRSue** preprint below (next-most relevant).

**Action:** Pull the medRxiv PDF; check whether they provide per-individual CIs or just rank-uncertainty. Also check the cross-PRS comparison — if methods like LDpred2, PRS-CS, and SBayesR are compared, that's the disagreement matrix you'd want to reproduce for cancer PRS in BioVU.

### 16. Causal Inference of Blood Pressure Reduction and Coronary Heart Disease Risk in the Framingham Study
**Authors:** Suchibrata Patra | **arXiv:** [2605.05666v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05666v1) | **Digest:** `2026-05-09.md`
**Thread fit:** Causal inference / pharmacoepi.

Applies Pearl's do-calculus to FHS Offspring (n=4,240). G-computation ACE for 20 mmHg SysBP reduction: **3.40% absolute risk reduction (95% CI: 2.64–4.14)** vs naïve observational **4.14%** — a ~22% relative *overestimation* of treatment benefit by standard risk calculators. Cross-corroborated with PS matching and IPW; CATE estimated via R/T-learners.

**Why it matters:** Concrete, clean case study showing how Framingham-style observational tools overestimate the absolute benefit of BP-lowering, with prescribing-threshold implications. Worth citing in any pharmacoepi writeup that compares observational risk to interventional effect estimates.

### 17. Correcting heterogeneous diagnostic bias when developing clinical prediction models using causal hidden Markov models
**Authors:** Benitez-Aurioles, Silva, McMillan, Sperrin | **arXiv:** [2605.06059v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06059v1) | **Digest:** `2026-05-09.md`
**Thread fit:** ML for precision health × EHR phenotyping × causal inference.

Targets the under-diagnosis bias that arises when high-risk groups are tested more frequently — induces label error, biasing both prediction models and validation metrics. Method: causal HMM with latent disease stage; defines counterfactual diagnosis-rate as the target estimand. CKD case study: corrects O:E ratio from 1.55 (1.51–1.59) to 1.01 (0.98–1.04) when predicting CKD diagnosis under counterfactual diagnostic rate.

**Why it matters:** Diagnostic-rate confounding is the right framing for many of your AoU / BioVU phenotyping problems (sex, ancestry, insurance status, all drive testing intensity). The estimand framing is principled and the calibration improvement is large enough to matter clinically.

**Action:** Read full paper; specifically look at how the latent-stage emission model is identified — that's the load-bearing assumption.
