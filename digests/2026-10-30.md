## Modular Bayesian Foundation Models: Bayesian time series encoders for OOD detection, routing and forecasting

**Authors:** Mame Diarra Toure, Quang Hung Pham, Mohamed Ryad Zemouri, David Stephens, Luc Vouligny, Martin Gagnon

**Primary category:** stat.ML

**arXiv:** [2610.39043v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39043v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-28

**Keyword hits:** foundation model

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Industrial systems continuously generate time series that vary in sampling frequency, scale, dynamics, and operating regime. Consequently a single time-series foundation model may not be able to represent all regimes effectively and retraining a large model for every new dataset or regime shift is computationally expensive. Modular foundation models address this issue by reusing specialised encoders trained on diverse datasets and by allowing the expansion of capabilities through the addition of new experts. This approach requires compact experts to minimize storage cost and a proper selection mechanism of experts for unseen data. We propose a modular foundation model framework that combines end-to-end Bayesian encoder training with uncertainty based routing. Using Singular Bayesian Neural Networks (SBNNs), we build a collection of compact Bayesian encoders whose epistemic uncertainty guides label-free selection. At the selected ranks, our Bayesian collection uses 63.8\% less storage than its deterministic counterpart. We evaluate this framework on time-series data through distribution shift, encoder selection, and probabilistic forecasting. Across seven incoming datasets, uncertainty-based routing is competitive with distance-based routing in terms of forecast MSE, while reducing average ECE from 0.086 to 0.072 and average NLL from 0.907 to 0.836. It also increases PICP90 from 82.28\% to 89.03\%. In a separate model-family comparison, we evaluate the SBNN pipeline against full-rank Bayes by Backprop, deep ensembles, and MC Dropout. The SBNN pipeline achieves an average MSE of 0.390, comparable with 0.396 for deep ensembles, 0.389 for MC dropout, and 0.443 for the full-rank Bayesian model. It uses 63.9\% fewer parameters than MC dropout, 81.9\% fewer than the full-rank model, and 92.8\% fewer than the deep ensemble.

## FACTS-MAS: A Multi-Agent Architecture for Adaptive Forecasting from Heterogeneous Evidence

**Authors:** Madumita Karthikeyan, Umang Rajnikant Bid, Rong Pan

**Primary category:** cs.AI

**arXiv:** [2610.38225v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38225v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-28

**Keyword hits:** foundation model

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Real-world forecasting often requires integrating heterogeneous evidence whose predictive value varies across horizons, operating regimes, and domains. We introduce FACTS-MAS, a domain-agnostic multi-agent forecasting framework that decomposes evidence into specialized channels for temporal dynamics, exogenous context, seasonality, structural information, and discrete events. Each agent generates an independently testable forecast through a common interface, while leakage-safe historical validation measures its predictive skill relative to a naive reference. These skill estimates are used to weight and combine agent forecasts, so that uninformative evidence is either removed or downweighted rather than forced to participate. A calibration procedure selects domain-specific constant means based on historical validation folds. We evaluate FACTS-MAS on weekly for-sale housing inventory from Zillow and weekly unemployment insurance claims from FRED, for 15 U.S. metropolitan areas at 4-, 8-, and 13-week forecasting horizons. Our method outperforms both a naive last-value forecast method and the TimesFM foundation model. However, a factor-adjusted exponential smoothing model performs as well as the fused forecast. The evidence therefore supports FACTS-MAS as a systematic protocol for testing, calibrating, and recomposing evidence channels before assembling them into the strongest single model for a specific application domain. Code, data, and results are available at https://github.com/Madumita24/FACTS-MAS.

## Weighting Scheme and Construction in IPCW Survival Estimation

**Authors:** Ruohui Chen, Shuhan Liu, Scott Zuo, Lihui Zhao, Hui Zhang, Xin Tu, Dung-Tsa Chen, Qianxing Mo

**Primary category:** stat.ME

**arXiv:** [2610.38102v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38102v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-28

**Keyword hits:** inverse probability

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Inverse probability of censoring weighting (IPCW) is widely used to address dependent censoring in survival analysis. Beyond estimation of the censoring distribution, its implementation requires specifying how inverse probability weights are incorporated into survival estimators. IPCW does not uniquely determine this construction, and different choices can produce different finite-sample behavior even under the same censoring model.
  In this work, we examine a class of IPCW estimators for the survival function, distinguishing hazard-based from margin-based formulations and time-varying from fixed weighting schemes. We show how these choices affect structural validity, stability, and finite-sample performance. In particular, the weight-changing hazard (WCH) estimator applies time-varying censoring weights within a product-limit construction with risk-set normalization, ensuring monotonicity and boundedness when the weighted risk-set denominators are positive. Through simulation studies under varying levels of covariate-dependent and administrative censoring, we demonstrate that WCH achieves favorable bias--variance tradeoffs and improved finite-sample stability relative to the alternative IPCW constructions examined, particularly in settings with few observed events or heavy censoring. We further illustrate these differences using data from the Women's Interagency HIV Study (WIHS), in which alternative IPCW constructions produced meaningfully different survival curves.
  Overall, our findings emphasize that the effectiveness of IPCW depends on both the weighting scheme and the estimator formulation. Careful consideration of these choices is therefore important for reliable survival estimation under dependent censoring.
