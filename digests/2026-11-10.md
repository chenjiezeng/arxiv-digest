## Does Predictive Performance Translate to Causal Performance? Evaluating tabular foundation models as nuisance learners for causal effect estimation

**Authors:** Andrew R Weckstein, Issa J Dahabreh, Marinka Zitnik, Sonia Hernández-Díaz, Sebastian Schneeweiss

**Primary category:** stat.AP

**arXiv:** [2611.11381v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11381v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-08

**Keyword hits:** propensity score, causal inference, foundation model

**Score:** 3

**Abstract:**

Tabular foundation models based on prior-data fitted networks (PFNs) achieve strong predictive performance, but their value for causal inference is unclear. We evaluated whether PFNs, which predict through in-context learning, could match or outperform conventional nuisance learners in supporting accurate causal estimation and reliable uncertainty quantification (inference). Learners modeled propensity scores and outcome regressions within doubly robust estimators of average causal effects, with and without cross-fitting, across synthetic and semi-synthetic settings with varying confounding complexity and effect heterogeneity.
  Under simple parametric data-generating processes, the PFNs were competitive with correctly specified parametric regressions and ensembles containing them. In more complex settings, they outperformed all conventional learners evaluated, including richly specified Super Learner ensembles, for both accuracy and uncertainty quantification. As sample size increased, only the two PFN models (TabPFN and Mitra) consistently approached nominal confidence interval coverage, whereas under-coverage persisted for conventional learners. In some settings, PFNs achieved near-nominal coverage and well-calibrated standard errors even without cross-fitting. At the smallest sample size (n=200) and in the higher-dimensional semi-synthetic settings, PFNs and other flexible learners exhibited poor coverage without cross-fitting. For PFNs, this appeared to be driven by propensity score overfitting consistent with in-sample memorization of treatment labels. These findings highlight the promise of prior-data fitted networks as in-context nuisance learners for causal estimation, while underscoring the need for further evaluation in challenging settings.

## Bias in Method-of-Moments Heritability Estimation Under Model Misspecification

**Authors:** Jonathan Gal, Sriram Sankararaman, Saharon Rosset

**Primary category:** stat.AP

**arXiv:** [2611.12615v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12615v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-09

**Keyword hits:** biobank

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Method-of-Moments (MoM) based approaches for estimation of variance components --- heritability and beyond --- are central in computational genetics due to their flexibility in addressing diverse and complex models while minimizing assumptions, and ease of computation and approximation in biobank-scale data. MoM are closely related to other important methods in this area like LD-score regression. A fundamental, highly debated question relates to the bias inherent in MoM estimates of variance components when the fitted model is misspecified: since the true underlying model is unknown, the fitted model may be missing some of the components from the true model or contain superfluous ones. We offer a general mathematical formulation of this bias and investigate its properties. This leads to novel insights about the sign and the magnitude of the bias in settings of interest and allows extensions and re-interpretation of previous MoM results in the literature. One case study we pursue is in estimating gene-environment interaction variance components (GxE). We prove that when omitting environment-specific noise components (NxE), the resulting GxE estimates are strictly upward biased, even when no true interaction exists. In another case study, we consider heritability estimation, when the genetic variance components are incorrectly partitioned (such as using a single genome-wide component instead of chromosome-specific components). We derive explicit expressions for the bias in the estimate of genome-wide heritability and its dependence on LD scores within and between components. For example, if all components have the same average LD score, the misspecified heritability estimate is still unbiased.
