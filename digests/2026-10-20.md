## Dirichlet-Tree Allocation Priors for Bayesian Causal Forests

**Authors:** Javier Enrique Aguilar, Daniel Klippert, Yusufhan Balci, Alexander Marx

**Primary category:** stat.ME

**arXiv:** [2610.26728v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26728v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-19

**Keyword hits:** causal forest, heterogeneous treatment effects

**Score:** 2

**Abstract:**

Bayesian Causal Forests (BCF) estimate heterogeneous treatment effects with a separate ensemble of treatment trees. The prior on the leaf values of the treatment trees determines whether sampling variability is attributed to the treatment effect. Independent shrinkage priors exert no control over how a tree allocates variability across its leaves, and their total prior variance grows as leaves are added. We propose the Dirichlet-Tree Bayesian Causal Forest, which jointly regularizes the leaf values of each treatment tree through a Dirichlet-Tree prior on their variance shares, generated by stick-breaking along the tree topology. We characterize the spike and tails of the marginal leaf prior, show that the prior adaptively bounds the number of regions of effect heterogeneity, and establish posterior consistency for the forest. In simulations, the model outperforms BCF when heterogeneity is confined to a few regions and reduces spurious heterogeneity elsewhere, and, unlike independent shrinkage priors, its accuracy does not deteriorate as the trees grow. We also evaluate the model on real data, including a pancreatic cancer survival study.

## Bombadil: A Foundation Model for Plant Electrophysiology

**Authors:** James Kirk, Piotr Bednarski, Filip Twardy, Justin Mast, Lee von Kraus

**Primary category:** cs.LG

**arXiv:** [2610.26597v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26597v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-19

**Keyword hits:** foundation model

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Living plants, like animals, use electrical signals to carry information about their physiological state across their body, from roots to branches. These signals can be recorded, as they are in animals, from a pair of differential extracellular electrodes inserted into signal-conducting tissue. While machine-learning models have been trained to predict specific plant stress states from these signals, each of these models has been trained for only a single task and none have been designed to transfer across tasks, species, or environments. These approaches do not allow easy scaling across the wide range of species and environments that are relevant to agriculture, forestry, and horticulture for both commercial and consumer plant care.
  Here we present Bombadil, a foundation model for plant electrophysiology, pretrained without labels on over 4 million hours of electrical signals recorded from 100 plant species in ten distinct environments, including laboratories, greenhouses, commercial orchards, and natural forests. In this way, Bombadil is able to learn the fundamental features of plant electrical signals across a wide array of species and environments. Then, from those same electrical signals, Bombadil tracks changes in seven physiological indicators of plant stress, even among species and stressors with no labels in training.

## Spontaneous brain-wide rotational dynamics constrain cue-evoked behavior

**Authors:** Sihan Chen, Yunlong Xu, Doruk Efe Gökmen, Michel Fruchart, Brent Doiron, Vincenzo Vitelli

**Primary category:** q-bio.NC

**arXiv:** [2610.25061v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25061v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-18

**Keyword hits:** motor

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Uncovering low-dimensional representations of brain-wide neural dynamics and their relationship to behavior remains a central challenge. Here, we show that spontaneous brain-wide rotational dynamics constrain cue-evoked behavior and learning. To systematically establish these findings, we developed a dimensionality-reduction technique called Rotational Component Analysis (RCA) that optimizes the detection of low-variance rotational modes even within individual trials. We applied RCA to motor-cortical spike-train data from monkeys and humans, mouse cortex-wide calcium imaging, and human functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) and magnetoencephalography (MEG). In mice, a rotational mode identified from activity recorded at rest predicts cue-evoked behavior: the phase of rotation when a randomly timed cue arrives predicts whether the animal subsequently pulls the lever. We explain this surprisingly strong correlation using both a recurrent neural network (RNN) and a minimal two-dimensional model of response learning, in which the intrinsic rotational mode serves as a dynamical constraint on response and learning. We further identify double-rotation modes containing two centers of rotation that separate preparatory from task dynamics in both mouse and human data. Remarkably, corresponding modes also organize neural dynamics during a non-motor human task, and rotational irreversibility captures early psychosis even when principal component analysis shows no group difference. Our work establishes rotational dynamics as low-dimensional coordinates that constrain how ongoing brain states are converted into behavior. More broadly, RCA provides a versatile tool for detecting hidden nonreciprocal dynamics in biological and physical datasets.
