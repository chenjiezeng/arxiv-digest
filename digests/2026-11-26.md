## Did the Model Forecast It, or Already Know It? Evaluating Large Language Models for Novel Drug-Target Prioritisation with a Temporal Knowledge-Graph Oracle

**Authors:** Pui Chung Siu, Yuan Liang, Claudia Cabrera, Mani Mudaliar, Arkaitz Zubiaga

**Primary category:** cs.AI

**arXiv:** [2611.34247v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34247v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-25

**Keyword hits:** knowledge graph

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

AI systems increasingly help decide which drug targets enter costly clinical trials. We recast each target-disease hypothesis as a dated event, the pair's first clinical trial. This lets us test whether a language model can forecast such events after its training cutoff, rather than recall events before it. We rebuild a dated biomedical knowledge graph as it stood at the end of each year from 2020 up to its newest version. We then ask six open language models, with and without this graph, which targets will enter a first trial for a disease the next year. Without retrieval, the models rank about as well as trial history, a count of each target's past trials in the same therapeutic area, also after their training cutoffs. Evidence dated before the decision adds at most a small gain, before or after the models' training cutoffs. The newest graph raises five of six models clearly above that baseline, but its paths usually include the answer: the pair's own trial, dated after the decision. Evaluations of target prioritisation should date both features and labels, with evidence from before the decision and outcomes after it.

## A Quantile-Based Framework for Bimodal Distribution Construction

**Authors:** Dreamlee Sharma, Nikita Rawat

**Primary category:** stat.ME

**arXiv:** [2611.33329v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33329v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-24

**Keyword hits:** latent class

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

This study suggests a novel quantile-based framework to construct bimodal distribution which does not rely on mixture representations or closed-form probability density functions. This proposed approach induces bimodality by transforming the quantile density through a strictly positive modulation function so that it can preserve the support and tail behavior of a chosen baseline distribution with providing explicit control over modal structure. The method is shown to apply broadly across bounded, semi-bounded and unbounded distribution families which include power, reflected power, logistic, Weibull and generalized lambda distributions. Simulation studies and a real-data application were performed which have shown that the proposed construction yields smooth, interpretable and computationally stable bimodal models and it also provides a flexible alternative to classical mixture-based approaches. Also this framework allows gradual transitions between unimodal and bimodal shapes which make it well suited for modeling overlapping regimes which are commonly encountered in real-world data. This proposed methodology therefor offers a robust and interpretable tool for bimodal modeling without requiring latent class assumptions or complex estimation procedures.
