## Documenting Trofinetide Treatment Phenotypes in Rett Syndrome with a Dual-LLM Consensus Pipeline

**Authors:** Daniel Palacios, Guantong Qi, Mikael Guzman Karlsson, Shawntel K. David, Mohammed Sameeruddin, Bernhard Suter, Zhandong Liu, Davut Pehlivan, Hu Chen

**Primary category:** q-bio.QM

**arXiv:** [2610.22062v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22062v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-15

**Keyword hits:** motor

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Objective: Trofinetide, approved in 2023 as the first Rett syndrome treatment, lacks scalable real-world monitoring because chart review is labor-intensive. We developed a dual large language model (LLM) consensus pipeline to extract 18 treatment-response phenotypes, comprising 4 adverse effects, 13 Rett clinical features, and medication refusal, from clinical notes. Materials and Methods: We analyzed 26,763 notes from 108 patients with confirmed sustained trofinetide exposure at Texas Children's Hospital. The pipeline filters notes by keyword, classifies each through two LLMs requiring consensus, and aggregates a patient-by-timepoint matrix. Architecture and prompt were selected on a diarrhea development set, then frozen and applied to the other 17 phenotypes with the phenotype definition substituted, as an out-of-sample test. The reported prompt comparison scores 183 expert-labeled cells. Results: A prompt requiring diarrhea to be active and severe performed best (84.2 $\pm$ 0.0% accuracy; F1 = 0.674), 47 percentage points above regular-expression matching ($p < 0.0001$). Expert reviewers judged six of the pipeline's calls per phenotype, three positive and three negative, and agreed with 90.2% (92/102). The 13 Rett clinical features are manifestations of the disorder itself rather than adverse effects of trofinetide. Gross and fine motor impairment, language/communication, and stereotypy were documented present in 92-100% of patients at every visit. Discussion: In-context LLM extraction captured multi-domain treatment responses. The outcome is documented presence at each visit, not a direction-of-change judgment. Conclusion: Coupling in-context LLM reasoning with a consensus architecture offers a scalable alternative to keyword and regular-expression extraction for real-world evidence, adapting to new phenotypes and medications through configuration alone.

## Panoramic modelling of single-cell transcriptomes at scale with CellNet

**Authors:** Xiaoqing Lian, Pei Liu, Jiahui Tan, Luoda Tan, Chaoyi Li, Quan Zou, Chen Lin, Xiangxiang Zeng

**Primary category:** q-bio.GN

**arXiv:** [2610.21943v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21943v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-15

**Keyword hits:** foundation model

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Comprehensively characterizing cellular diversity is critical for understanding tissue development, identifying disease-associated populations and investigating gene programmes linked to specialized functions. Advances in single-cell profiling reveal additional cell-specific transcripts and molecular associations, yet many foundation models represent only a restricted portion of this increasingly rich molecular information. Here we present CellNet, which enables panoramic modelling of single-cell transcriptomes by integrating DNA-derived gene identities and measured RNA expression in a shared image representation. Pretrained on 231 million cell profiles spanning 40 species, CellNet improves rare-population and condition-associated state recognition in evaluated cohorts while retaining competitive general cell representations. It predicts withheld coding-gene expression and selected non-coding targets in total-RNA data, and recovers lineage-specific expression patterns in spatial measurements. Prediction also extends across species, including to targets without expression-pretraining exposure. Its gene representations retrieve known non-coding modules and developmental or neural programmes, including from sparsely detected RNA queries. Together, these results establish CellNet as a scalable framework for interpreting cellular diversity through increasingly comprehensive transcriptomic measurements.

## Poisoning the corpora of protein design systems during pre-training and retrieval

**Authors:** Charalampos Koilakos, Kimon-Antonios Provatas, Michail Patsakis, Candace SY Chan, Georgios A. Pavlopoulos, Ilias Georgakopoulos-Soares

**Primary category:** cs.LG

**arXiv:** [2610.21200v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21200v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-15

**Keyword hits:** foundation model

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Protein foundation models increasingly support the generation of novel biological sequences, yet their reliance on large public datasets creates opportunities for adversarial manipulation of the material they learn from and retrieve. We investigate two data-only poisoning attacks against protein design systems, targeting the pre-training corpus of autoregressive protein language models and the retrieval corpus of a text-guided protein generator. For the first, we pre-trained ProtGPT2 and ProGen2 variants of 300 million parameters, introducing poisoned protein sequences containing one of two triggers followed by one of three payloads. Across all poisoned runs, both architectures acquired highly reliable trigger-dependent generation under uniform and ramped poisoning schedules. Under ramped exposure, the earliest majority activation occurred after only 200 contaminated examples, while for the uniform schedule earliest activation occurred around 5000 poisoned samples. For the second attack, we inserted records into a retrieval corpus system without modifying its weights or decoding procedure. A single query-aware record captured a top-16 retrieval position for 58\% of templated requests and 97\% of free-text requests. Conditional on successful poisoned retrieval, 62\% of requests produced altered sequences, while 19-21% acquired the inserted domain by profile-HMM homology. A universal query-unaware attack using 1,500 records also achieved substantial retrieval capture and domain transfer. Together, these findings demonstrate that adversarial protein sequence contributions can establish persistent, sequence-specific behaviour during pre-training or redirect protein generation at inference, while leaving ordinary model behaviour largely preserved. Our work establishes the integrity and biological provenance of protein sequence data as critical safeguards for generative protein design systems.
