## Shape-Preserving Treatment Effects for Functional Outcomes

**Authors:** Agathe Chabassier, Joris Olivier, Julie Josse, Erwan Scornet

**Primary category:** stat.AP

**arXiv:** [2610.35180v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35180v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-26

**Keyword hits:** causal inference, glp-1

**Score:** 2

**Abstract:**

By recording health measurements daily rather than at a few clinical visits, connected devices pave the way for more precise real-world evidence that measures how the effect of an intervention unfolds over time: does the response rebound, peak late, or oscillate? Yet causal methods for such functional outcomes remain scarce, and those that exist target the Functional Average Treatment Effect (FATE). Its pointwise averaging flattens the features patients share whenever their curves are misaligned in time, as is common with biosignals, where patients respond with their own timing. We develop a causal inference framework that captures the shape of the effect of a binary intervention, based on the phase-centered elastic mean from elastic shape analysis. We lift this mean from a sample statistic to a functional of the data-generating law and prove that it is well defined under explicit geometric assumptions. Because it is not linear, it yields three causal estimands instead of one: populational, individual and local. We identify the populational estimand by inverse-probability weighting and the local estimand through the conditional outcome laws, estimate them by weighting and by matching, and show that the individual one is not identifiable. On simulations with timing variability or heterogeneous effect shapes, our estimators recover the shape that the FATE loses. On real weight trajectories following GLP-1 receptor agonist initiation, they reveal a rebound that the FATE averages away. This framework thus gives clinicians a treatment-effect curve whose shape and timing reflect genuine patient trajectories rather than a misalignment artifact.

## Accuracy and efficiency of compact foundation models for surface-water mapping during floods

**Authors:** Parastoo Farajpoor, Mohammadreza Narimani

**Primary category:** eess.IV

**arXiv:** [2610.34904v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34904v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-26

**Keyword hits:** chip, foundation model

**Score:** 2

**Abstract:**

Pretrained Earth observation models may reduce the effort needed to map surface water, but their value depends on the alternative against which they are compared. We evaluated Prithvi-EO-2.0-Tiny using the 446 hand-labeled Sentinel-2 chips from 11 flood events in Sen1Floods11. A fine-tuned model, a frozen-encoder model, and an identical randomly initialized architecture were compared with a similarly sized U-Net, a larger Prithvi model, and spectral-index thresholds. Experiments varied the number of training chips, excluded events from model fitting, and measured computational cost and prediction reliability. With 252 training chips, U-Net achieved a water intersection-over-union (IoU) of 0.856 on the official test set, compared with 0.833 for fine-tuned Prithvi-Tiny. Pretraining improved the tiny transformer over random initialization by a reported 0.046 IoU with 25 training chips and 0.023 with 252 chips, but did not reverse the U-Net ranking. Across 11 event-exclusion evaluations, mean IoU was 0.768 for fine-tuned Prithvi-Tiny and 0.777 for U-Net; the paired interval included zero. Fine-tuned Prithvi-Tiny processed a 512x512-pixel chip in 16.1 ms on a GPU and 258 ms on a laptop CPU, compared with 30.6 and 665 ms for U-Net. Errors were concentrated near water boundaries, and high predictive entropy identified many, but not all, erroneous pixels. These results support a specific role for compact pretrained models: lower measured computational demand with competitive accuracy, rather than consistent superiority. Event-dependent performance, shared hyperparameter selection, and the distinction between observed water and newly inundated land delimit the conclusions.

## Resolving the Mobility-Equity-Emissions Trilemma: A Causal Machine Learning Framework for Dynamic Tolling Policy

**Authors:** Christian Mulomba Mukendi

**Primary category:** cs.LG

**arXiv:** [2610.33994v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33994v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-25

**Keyword hits:** double machine learning

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

We propose a causal machine learning framework to integrate dynamic tolling for mobility, equity, and climate objectives, resolving a long-standing policy trilemma. Using Double Machine Learning, we estimate zone-type-specific treatment effects, showing that commercial zones yield nearly twice the delay reduction of residential zones. Our adaptive quota mechanism expands residential coverage 34-fold while preserving 93% of total delay savings and sustaining high return on investment. The framework delivers climate co-benefits, reducing emissions by 743.5 metric tons of CO2 (valued at 141 USD) via congestion-sensitive pricing under moderate traffic conditions with sensitivity bounds from 557.6 to 929.3 tons. Crucially, we uncover a policy-invariant toll range where performance remains robust across demand scenarios, simplifying implementation. This study reveals critical, previously unidentified insights, such as a policy-invariant toll range, that can significantly simplify real-world implementation. Validated on synthetic data mirroring real-world networks, our methodological advances lay the foundation for future public deployment.

## The Computational Assessment Stack: An Architecture for AI-Native Measurement

**Authors:** Alina A. von Davier

**Primary category:** stat.AP

**arXiv:** [2610.33901v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33901v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-10-24

**Keyword hits:** knowledge graph

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Test development is shifting toward automation and agentic artificial intelligence (AI), in which autonomous systems draft items at scale, assemble test forms, administer adaptive assessments, score complex responses, and monitor test quality. As autonomous systems take on more of this work, the architecture underlying test development must change to preserve assessment quality under the new paradigm. This paper proposes the Computational Assessment Stack (CAS), a four-layer architecture for AI-native measurement where every stage of the assessment lifecycle shares a unifying computational construct representation as its base layer. This representation is a machine-readable, modular, and versioned knowledge graph. Three layers are built above it: computational content generation that builds on the knowledge graph; computational psychometrics that derives substantive structure directly from the knowledge graph; an orchestration layer in which agentic systems operationalize the stack under human governance. The paper's core contribution is architectural. Building on foundational psychometric frameworks, the CAS provides a persistent representation that unifies the four layers. Unlike the descriptive prose traditionally used to define constructs in test development, the proposed architecture renders measurement assumptions inspectable and their consequences empirically testable.
