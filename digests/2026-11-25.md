## Cancer multi-omics joint dimension reduction leads to reliable and interpretable survival predictions

**Authors:** Vincent Le Goff, Vincent Guillemot, Cathy Philippe, Wiam Ahdidou, Gwendoline Mendes, Jean-François Deleuze, Edith Le Floch, Arnaud Gloaguen

**Primary category:** q-bio.GN

**arXiv:** [2611.32382v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32382v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-24

**Keyword hits:** patient stratification

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Multi-omics studies are becoming more and more frequent, and are especially suited to decrypt the complexity of dysregulation happening in cancer. In this context, improving prognostication through interpretable methods could provide better patient stratification and help identify new prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets. Multi-omics data are however very often high-dimensional (few patients/high number of variables). A possible solution to high-dimensionality is Joint Dimension Reduction (JDR) methods, which compute a low-dimensional latent space from multi-omics data. In this work, we benchmark 12 JDR methods on 18 different TCGA cancer datasets. Survival prediction is achieved through a two-step process: the estimation of a low-dimensional multi-omics latent space followed by survival prediction with or without additional clinical data. We had to equip 4 methods with the ability to predict the survival of new patients. Results reveal that JDR methods can improve survival prediction with clinical data, and match the predictive performance of clinical data alone, with a high variability between different cancers. Among JDR methods, JIVE and RGCCA show superior performance. In a subsequent bootstrap analysis, JDR methods identify reliable prognostic biomarkers across cancers. We propose a clinically relevant interpretation of such markers on Low-Grade Glioma. Reproducible codes are available on https://github.com/vlegoff/MultiOBenchmark.

## Differential Item Functioning: Methodological Advances in Detection, Identification, and Interpretation

**Authors:** Gabriel Wallin, Qi Huang

**Primary category:** stat.ME

**arXiv:** [2611.31696v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31696v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-23

**Keyword hits:** latent class

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Differential item functioning (DIF) occurs when an item's response distribution differs across groups for respondents at the same level of the construct. Separating DIF from group differences in the construct itself requires identifying assumptions. Conventional analyses compare observed groups on a common scale established through items assumed to be invariant. We distinguish four settings according to whether the groups are observed or latent and whether the common scale is fixed by prespecified restrictions or estimated from the data. For observed groups, we review observed-score and item response theory procedures, multiple-covariate and intersectional models, and recursive partitioning. When anchors are unknown, we compare empirical anchor selection, partial and approximate invariance, robust scaling, regularisation and sparsity-based identification by the assumptions each makes about the pattern of DIF. For latent groups, we discuss mixture models and the joint estimation of class membership and DIF. Further topics include ordinal and multidimensional models, inference after data-dependent selection, effect sizes, consequences for composite scores, and the sources of DIF. We conclude with open problems, including anchor-free identification when DIF is widespread and inference when latent classes and invariant items are selected jointly.

## Tempering and few-shot calibration of pathology foundation models improve digital biomarker reproducibility across slide scanners

**Authors:** Frank T Wen, Kyler Rosen, Siddhi Ramesh, Michael Koo, Sara Kochanny, Sasha Jain-Liu, Emma Dyer, Alexander T Pearson, James M Dolezal

**Primary category:** q-bio.QM

**arXiv:** [2611.31536v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31536v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-23

**Keyword hits:** foundation model

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Pathology foundation models underpin a growing number of histology-based cancer biomarkers, but sensitivity to slide scanner-specific image signatures degrades cross-center reliability and reproducibility. We developed a foundation model fine-tuning method that improved consistency in head-and-neck cancer human papillomavirus (HPV) prediction, colorectal cancer microsatellite instability (MSI) identification, prostate cancer grading and breast cancer phenotyping. This tempering method decreased cross-scanner prediction differences by 67.5% for HPV (95% CI 59.0-73.9%), 69.3% for MSI (95% CI 51.9-76.5%) and 63.4%-81.2% for breast signatures. Cross-scanner prostate grade agreement rose from 42.5% to 92.7%. Under full scanner-HPV confounding during training, AUROC fell from 0.80 to 0.68 with original model features but only from 0.86 to 0.84 with tempering. Unlike post hoc feature calibration, tempering used only public data yet matched or exceeded on-site calibration. These findings support tempering to reduce scanner dependence of pathology foundation models.

## A multimodal foundation model for tissue proteomics and morphology in cancer

**Authors:** Benedikt von Querfurth, Lukas Klein, Cédric Vincent-Cuaz, Eeshaan Jain, Yexiang Cheng, Johann Wenckstern, Phil F. Cheng, Petros Liakopoulos, Olivier Michielin, Martina Haberecker, Andreas Wicki, Pascal Frossard, Charlotte Bunne

**Primary category:** q-bio.QM

**arXiv:** [2611.31451v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31451v1)

**Submitted:** 2026-11-23

**Keyword hits:** foundation model

**Score:** 1

**Abstract:**

Cancer diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment decisions increasingly rely on integrating tissue morphology with specific molecular readouts of the tumor and its microenvironment. H&E histopathology provides scalable morphology, whereas multiplex spatial proteomics quantifies dozens of proteins in situ with spatial context, but remains selectively deployed, often with different marker panels across cohorts. This creates a translational bottleneck: most patients lack high-depth molecular profiling, and existing models rarely integrate morphology and spatial molecular data in a single framework. We present VirTues-M2, a multimodal foundation model that jointly learns from H&E and spatial proteomics using a novel vision transformer-based architecture. By encoding marker identity during pretraining, VirTues-M2 is robust to missing modalities, imperfect modality pairing or registration, and variable marker panels, enabling unified inference for tissue phenotyping, panoptic cell segmentation, virtual staining, annotation-free biomarker discovery, and reinforcement learning-based adaptive marker panel selection to reduce assay burden. As a concrete demonstration of translational utility, in a newly collected cohort of 1,053 treatment-naive non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients with paired spatial proteomics and H&E, VirTues-M2 discovers high-risk and low-risk tissue signatures that stratify overall survival in both the discovery cohort and an independent validation cohort, outperforming current state-of-the-art stratification schemes; all enabled by VirTues-M2's ability to jointly analyze both modalities within a single model.
