### 1. GLP-1 receptor agonist use and liver-related outcomes in MASLD and type 2 diabetes in the All of Us Research Program

**Authors:** R. T. Chung (corresponding); All of Us cohort.

**Venue:** American Journal of Gastroenterology, 2026.

**Source:** Scholar alert, "All of Us research program" (2026-05-18).

**Thread fit:** GLP-1 drug-class thread + biobank/EHR thread (AoU). Sits
squarely at the intersection of pharmacoepidemiology and EHR-linked
biobank analysis — both core priorities in `INTERESTS.md`.

**What the snippet says:** Liver-related outcomes in MASLD and T2D
patients exposed to GLP-1 RAs in All of Us; mentions NIH AoU
acknowledgements. Full abstract not retrievable through Gmail MCP.

**Why it matters:** Real-world AoU data on a tracked drug class
(GLP-1) for a high-prevalence liver outcome. The methods question is
whether the authors used target trial emulation / IPW or a simpler
exposure-comparator design — that determines how much you can trust
the effect estimate vs. confounding by indication.

**Action:** Pull the AJG paper. Check (a) exposure window definition
(new-user vs. prevalent-user), (b) comparator (active comparator like
DPP-4i, or untreated reference), (c) outcome ascertainment (ICD-10
codes vs. lab-confirmed MASLD progression), (d) AoU subgroup sizes
for advanced fibrosis / cirrhosis. If active-comparator with IPW,
cite alongside SGLT2i/GLP-1 work in any drug-class pharmacoepi
writeup.

### 3. Characterizing the Uncertainty, Misclassification and Inconsistency of Polygenic Prediction

**Authors:** Y. Zhang, R. Zhang, T. Ge.

**Venue:** medRxiv, 2026.

**Source:** Scholar alert, "Lisa Bastarache - new related research"
(2026-05-18).

**Thread fit:** Genetic epidemiology (PRS) + ML for precision health
(calibration / individualized risk).

**Triage:** HIGH.

**What the snippet says:** "Polygenic risk scores (PRSs) hold promise
for precision medicine…" Paper characterizes uncertainty,
misclassification, and inconsistency in polygenic prediction at the
individual level.

**Why it matters:** This is the missing piece in nearly all PRS-based
clinical decision papers — most report population AUC, not individual
predictive uncertainty or test-retest inconsistency across PGS
catalogue updates. Directly relevant to the composite-risk-model
thread (PRS stacked with rare pathogenic variants), since
misclassification in the PRS component dominates the composite at
the tails.

**Action:** Read in full. Specifically check: how do they quantify
between-method inconsistency (PRS-CS vs. LDpred2 vs. SBayesR)? Do
they decompose uncertainty into LD reference, summary-stat noise, and
ancestry mismatch? If they propose a calibration metric, evaluate
folding it into the PheRS calibration writeup tracked from the
2026-05-12 supplement.

### 13. Discovery of Hidden Miscalibration Regimes

**Authors:** K. Kobalczyk, M. van der Schaar.

**Venue:** arXiv preprint arXiv:2605.13484, 2026.

**Source:** Scholar alert, "Mihaela van der Schaar - new articles"
(2026-05-18).

**Thread fit:** ML for precision health — calibration / decision-curve
analysis.

**Triage:** METHODS-WATCH.

**Why it matters:** Standard calibration metrics (Brier, ECE,
calibration intercept/slope) are aggregate — they miss subgroup
regimes where the model is systematically over/under-confident. If
the proposed method identifies miscalibration regimes a priori (vs.
post hoc subgroup discovery), it's potentially useful for fairness
audits of clinical models across ancestry / age / site.

**Action:** Read the methods. Check whether the regime discovery
needs subgroup labels at training time or can run unsupervised.
